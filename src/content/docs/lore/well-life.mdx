### Social Hierarchy
Life in the Well is ordered according to the Great Cycle. Its principles shape architecture, labor, education, and aspiration.

Hierarchy is not merely social - it is spatial.

A citizen's level of residence defines not only their physical position within the Well, but their place within its moral and civic structure. Proximity to the Sun at the crown is associated with refinement, authority, and purity. Distance from it implies burden, labor, and spiritual coarseness.

The language of elevation and descent is used interchangeably for both movement and status.

### The Rings
Rings function as currency, but they also serve as visible markers of caste.

The metal one carries - copper, silver, or gold - instantly signals one's social standing. Displaying rings openly is customary. Among mixed groups, a higher metal commands immediate deference.

Concealing one's wealth may be interpreted as evasive or subversive. In the Uppers, appearing without visible gold is often taken as admission that one does not belong. In the Lowers, an unexpected flash of silver can silence a room.

In the Well, status is worn.

### Social Mobility
The Great Cycle teaches that the soul may ascend through purification and devotion, drawing nearer to the Sun in this life and Xod in the next.

In practice, upward mobility is rare.

Most citizens spend their entire lives within a few levels of where they were born. Travel beyond one's layer is unusual unless required by occupation. Couriers, inspectors, and agricultural laborers working demiplane farms are among the few who routinely cross boundaries.

Demotion is far more common than promotion.

To be unringed and reassigned to a lower level is amongst the gravest punishments imposed by the state. Exile downward is not merely economic - it is spiritual, a formal declaration that one has fallen from favor and from light.

### Guild Life
From an early age, children are expected to know their place within the structure.

Basic schools, found every few levels, provide instruction in literacy, numeracy, doctrine, and practical skills. The curriculum reflects the needs of the surrounding levels; children in the industrial tiers are taught different trades than those in administrative districts.

Upon completing foundational schooling, most youths enter apprenticeship within their assigned guild. The duration of this apprenticeship varies by trade and performance, but full guild membership typically marks the beginning of lifelong service.

Movement within a guild is possible as positions open. Transfer between guilds is rare and often requires exceptional circumstance or intervention.

In the Well, vocation is destiny more often than choice.

### Species Within The Well
The Well is predominantly humanoid, with humans forming the majority of the population. Over generations, however, an increasing number of individuals have been born exhibiting unusual abilities or physical deviations from the norm. Scholars within the guilds study these mutations extensively, but no consensus exists as to their origin.

In addition, various species native to the demiplanes have gradually taken residence within the Well. Some began as curiosities - singular beings encountered during agricultural or exploratory assignments. Over time, small populations established themselves within certain levels.

More perplexing still are reports of awakened flora and fauna originating in the demiplanes. These beings have appeared intermittently over the years and now constitute a small but persistent minority.

While such individuals are no longer unheard of, acceptance is uneven. Some citizens regard them as neighbors, while others view them as interlopers, burdens, or potential threats to the stability of the Well.

Even within a single level, stratification persists. Guild proximity to the Chancellor and to the Ascendant carries prestige, while labor further removed from administrative or spiritual authority is deemed less refined.
