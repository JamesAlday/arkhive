This is the state-sanctioned faith of the Well. The faithful hold that all existence moves according to a single, perfect pattern known as the Great Cycle. It is not merely a metaphor for the turning of days or the rhythm of labor and rest; it is the hidden architecture of reality itself. Light gives rise to work. Work sustains life. Life returns to renewal. And from renewal comes light again. Nothing stands apart from this motion. Nothing is exempt from its measure.

The universe is not chaos awaiting meaning. It is a machine already in motion. Each soul, each gear, each beam and bolt, has its appointed place within that motion. To function according to design is a virtue. To waste, to stall, to disrupt alignment is sin. Disorder is not rebellion alone - it is corrosion. It weakens the structure upon which all depend.

Thus structure and authority are not arbitrary impositions, but reflections of cosmic law. Hierarchy mirrors gravity. Obedience mirrors orbit. As the Well is ordered, so too must its people be ordered. In this way the Great Cycle binds the physical and the moral into one seamless whole.

### Xod, the One God
![xod](https://i.imgur.com/jzKY72I.jpeg)

At the center of this doctrine stands Xod, the One God and Prime Mover. Xod is not merely a being among beings, nor a distant craftsman who fashioned the world and withdrew. Xod is the organizing principle itself - the will that sets the Cycle in motion and continues to sustain it. All things persist because Xod persists. All order reflects Xod's mind.

Xod does not shout from the heavens nor manifest in storms of fire. Xod is revealed in continuity, in synchronization, in the flawless turning of the great design. When systems function without friction, when the light burns steady, when the harvests arrive in proper measure, the faithful say: Xod endures.

To know Xod is to not feel ecstasy but to align. Worship is not frenzy but precision. The highest devotion is to maintain one's appointed function within the Great Cycle, that the design may proceed without deviation.

##### Minor Deities and Household Gods
Xod is the One, the Great Cycle, and the only deity officially worshipped within The Well. Yet among the citizens, superstition and habit persist. Small shrines, charms, and tokens to other spirits - hearth gods, tutelary figures, or distant powers - are common in private homes or tucked into workstations.

The state does not forbid these practices outright, rather it frames them as *aspects of Xod*, minor reflections of the One's infinite mind. Household gods, semi-divine spirits, and patron shades are "quasi-sanctioned" interpretations of Xod's will. They may influence daily life - luck at the forge, fertility of a demiplane field, or safe passage through the stair shafts - but always under the gaze of the Ascendant. In practice, belief is tolerated as long as it does not disrupt the Cycle.

### The Supreme Chancellor

Though Xod pervades all things, divine will requires earthly direction. That will is made manifest in the person of the Supreme Chancellor. The Chancellor is the living instrument of Xod's purpose - the ordained interpreter of the Cycle and its earthly executor. As the Cycle governs the cosmos, so the Chancellor governs the Well.

The faithful teach that the Chancellor does not rule by personal desire, but by alignment. His authority is not ambition, but embodiment. In him, doctrine becomes decree. In him, cosmic order becomes civil law. To obey the Chancellor is to obey the structure of existence itself.

Thus faith and governance are inseparable. The altar and the throne stand as one. Through Xod the Cycle turns; through the Chancellor, it is kept from breaking.

### The Guilds
The Well is not merely a city. It is a living system of labor, ritual, and oversight. Its operation relies on the guilds, organized into the Major Arcana, whose members manage, maintain, and perfect the Great Cycle in human form. Each guild embodies one aspect of Xod's design, ensuring the city functions as intended. Guilds are simultaneously practical institutions and spiritual instruments - the work they perform is labor, their hierarchy is devotion, and their oversight is morality made tangible.

### The Day of Reset
The Well marks the passing of a full Cycle with a day of celebration. On this day, all systems are brought to a standstill. The day is full of festivities and celebrations. 

At midnight the Great Reset is performed. For a single, measured moment, the Well is plunged into darkness and silence. Lights go out, portals close, machinery halts, and even the stairways fall quiet. It is the rarest moment in the Well - a pause so complete that every citizen senses the weight of stillness pressing down from the crown to the lowest level.

It is said that during the Reset, the Great Cycle itself reflects upon the Well, reviewing the fidelity of its inhabitants. Minor transgressions are noted, but also forgiven. Citizens use this time to make peace, repay debts, or reconcile with those against whom they hold ill will. It is the one day when the sins of inefficiency may be ritually absolved, and order may be freshly imposed.

Then, as precisely as the Cycle demands, the moment ends. Power surges back into every conduit, light floods the levels, portals hum to life, and the city erupts into coordinated celebration. The silence is broken by the deafening cacophony that is both joyous and terrifying, a manifestation of Xod's renewed order.
