The Well is a vast cylindrical city standing 140 levels tall beneath a shining crown. Each level rises 40 feet from floor to ceiling, forming an immense stack of chambers connected by a monumental central stair. 

The core of the cylinder is a yawning 160 foot wide open shaft, within which the grand stair spirals downward in a 60 foot wide ring. At the very center, past the inner wall of the stairs is a hollow core, 20 feet in diameter, that drops unbroken into the depths of the Well below.

### Levels
The Well descends through 140 levels, divided into three broad layers: the Uppers, the Mids, and the Lowers. Travel between the layers is not forbidden - but it is noticed. Most citizens spend their entire lives within ten levels of their birthplace. Movement beyond one's layer invites questions, paperwork, or suspicion, unless one's guildwork demands it.

#### Uppers
**Levels 1-20**
  - Home of the Chancellor's Estate
  - Spacious, quiet, and richly appointed.
  - Mostly single-story residences
  - Vast non-residential ceremonial and administrative spaces
  - Imports most of its manual labor from lower levels (live-in staff or long rotations)
  
  The Uppers are defined by space. Ceilings feel higher here, corridors broader, light cleaner. Sound carries differently - footsteps echo, sounds carry. Work performed here is administrative, ceremonial, or intellectual; physical labor is rarely local.
  
Gold rings are commonplace. Platinum is only seen in large transactions or displays of wealth.

#### Mids
**Levels 21-100**
  - Though officially treated as a single layer, the Mids function as two distinct social and architectural bands.
  
##### The Upper Mids
**Levels 21-50**
- Primarily 2-3 floor residential townhouses and apartment blocks
- Reliable amenities and modest comfort
- Limited recreational demiplane access
- Bureaucrats, analysts, spell-engineers, records clerks

The Upper Mids are orderly and efficient. Streets are narrower than above, but still structured. Residents here consider themselves stable, respectable, and upward-facing.

Silver predominates, with the occasional gold.

##### The Lower Mids
**Levels 51-100**
- Dense housing, often 3-4 stacked floors per level
- Industry mixed in with housing
- Primary industrial center of the Well
- Largest concentration of agricultural demiplane gates
- Factory workers, gate technicians, logistic planners, food processors
- Pulls in significant day labor from the Lowers

Here the hum of machinery is constant. Shifts rotate without pause. Housing grows tighter, streets busier, and the boundary between home and work blurs.

Silver fades toward copper.

#### Lowers
**Levels 101-140**
  - Crowded, loud, and dim.
  - Four stacked residential tiers per level
  - Heavy industry dominates, mixed in with residences
  - Power generation, waste reclamation, 
scrap processing, nutrient recovery
  
The Lowers carry the weight of the Well. The air smells faintly metallic. Machinery vibrates through the floors. Light is functional rather than aesthetic. Few from above descend without purpose.

Copper is common. Silver is rare. Gold draws attention.


### The Central Stairs
**The Central Stairs** are the primary artery of the Well - a continuous spiral stairway linking all 140 levels. Every landing opens onto its respective tier, making the stair both roadway and gathering place. With roughly one hundred steps between each level, even short journeys are measured in effort.

Travel times listed below assume light traffic. In practice, the stairs are rarely empty. Shift changes, market days, courier routes, and festival traffic can  reduce movement to a crawl. During peak hours, ascending is slower and descending requires caution.

Custom and courtesy matter here. Those traveling upward typically keep to the inner curve and those descending to the outer rail. Couriers announce themselves loudly. Veiled Hand patrols expect the path to clear.

Sound carries strangely. Conversations drift downward, arguments echo upward.

**The Central Shaft** lies at the heart of the spiral - a hollow cylinder running the entire height of the Well. Each landing includes a reinforced access door opening into this vertical void.

The shaft serves as the Well's primary refuse drop. Broken tools, spoiled goods, industrial waste, and household discards are consigned to the darkness below. The Lowers are tasked with sorting, reclaiming, and repurposing what survives the fall.

Dropping refuse is permitted. Dropping anything else is not.

Falling objects can reach dangerous speeds. During heavy disposal periods, warning bells may sound before large industrial dumps. Children are warned never to lean too far inward.

It is said that nothing truly vanishes in the Well - it merely descends.

### Lighting
The interior lattice of the Well is threaded with light-emitting crystals embedded within the walls and ceilings of every level. These crystals remain inert until activated by a simple geometric sigil traced upon the surrounding surface. The sigil is common knowledge; nearly anyone can draw it. Illumination, however, is not uniformly maintained.

Most levels maintain public lighting that follows a day/night cycle. The timing is regulated and synchronized across entire layers, creating shared hours of labor and rest. Private lighting varies according to wealth, access, and material availability.

At the crown of the Well is found The Sun, a brilliant central light burns constantly, casting illumination down the entire 140-level central shaft. From the upper levels, it shines a constant bright white that permeates most of the open areas of the level. By the time its light reaches the Lowers, it is pale and indirect, sufficient only near the inner railing. Deeper levels rely heavily on localized crystal-light and industrial glow.

### "Outside"/Demiplanes
Everything that has ever happened happened inside the Well. 

Unless it happened  outside.

Throughout the Well stand arcane doorways, reinforced frames of stone and sigil-work that open, when properly activated, onto contained demiplanes. These pocket realms serve as the Well's fields, forests, quarries, and preserves. Nearly all produce, meat, timber, ore, and other raw materials consumed within the Well originate beyond these thresholds.

The demiplanes are not wilderness in the conventional sense. They are controlled environments: measured acreage, bounded horizons, regulated climates. Some are pastoral and sunlit. Others are harsh, mineral-rich, or deliberately austere. Each is cataloged, scheduled, and assigned.

Access to the demiplanes is tightly managed. Doorways are locked, sealed, and overseen according to guild authority. Travel requires authorization, and shifts are timed precisely. Most agricultural demiplanes are accessed from the Lower Mids, though much of the labor comes from the Lowers, who ascend daily to their assigned gates before descending again at shift's end.

To most citizens, "outside" does not mean freedom. It means assignment.

Recreational demiplanes exist in limited numbers and are generally restricted to the Upper Mids and above. These are smaller, curated spaces - gardens, artificial shores, preserved woodlands - and are granted as a privilege rather than a right.

Few in the Well have ever seen an unbounded horizon. Fewer still believe such a thing exists.

### Currency
The Well uses the standard D&D denominations of precious metals: Platinum, Gold, Silver, and Copper, but does not mint coins. Instead, all currency takes the form of consecrated metal rings.

Each ring is stamped, blessed, and recorded under the authority of the Ascendant. The money supply is tightly controlled, and all raw materials originate from secured demiplane mines. Tampering with the money supply is not merely illegal - it is both treason and heresy.

Rings are most commonly worn on a leather loop at the belt or as a necklace. Wealth is visible. Movement between levels often means movement between currencies.

#### Copper
![copper](https://i.imgur.com/y2Jz7un.jpeg)

Copper rings are the standard currency on the Lower levels. They are typically thicker and heavier than higher denominations, built for durability and constant exchange. Copper circulates
rapidly and widely; it is the sound of markets, labor halls, and factory floors.


For many citizens of the Well, copper is the only metal they will ever regularly handle.

#### Silver
![copper](https://i.imgur.com/9fMkv3x.jpeg)

Silver dominates the Mids. Among groups of copper-bearing workers, even a single silver ring signals authority or advancement. Many laborers from the Lowers travel upward to the Lower Mids for industrial or agricultural shifts, where silver is commonplace and copper is expected.

Silver represents stability, skill, and modest elevation.


#### Gold
![copper](https://i.imgur.com/n5AVc8q.jpeg)

Gold rings are the standard currency of the Upper levels. Here they are not merely carried, but displayed, worn as ornament as much as tender. Gold circulates more slowly and in smaller quantities than other metals.

The lower one is born in the Well, the less likely one is to have seen gold in open exchange. When gold descends, it rarely does so without escort.


#### Platinum
![copper](https://x42solutions.blob.core.windows.net/content-repository/39b2a884-6656-4e5d-8124-cc66e1753b08.png)
Platinum is not used in daily trade. No rings are struck from it. Instead, platinum exists as vault-weight bars reserved for the Chancellor and large institutional transfers. It functions as a store of sovereignty rather than a medium of exchange.

It is highly likely that most citizens will never see platinum outside of the ceremony.

#### Authority and Control
The Ascendant guild oversees the minting and consecration of all rings. Mining operations are confined to tightly controlled demiplanes, and the movement of raw metal is guarded.

Unauthorized minting, defacement, alloy tampering, or counterfeiting are treated as acts against both state and doctrine.

### Population
The Well is home to countless citizens, but exact numbers are never cited. From above, it can feel spacious and orderly; from the lower levels, it can feel crowded and noisy. Population density varies by layer: the Uppers are sparsely populated, with wide corridors and private residences; the Mids are bustling and layered with residential, industrial, and commercial zones; the Lowers are dense, loud, and always in motion.

Citizens rarely move far from their level of birth, which reinforces social stratification and a sense of belonging, or confinement. While humans are most common, the Well also hosts those who bear mutations, and over time, creatures from the demiplanes have slowly integrated. The Well is always alive - footsteps echo on the stairwells, voices carry through the halls, and the hum of industry and portal activity never fully ceases.

This layered population is more than a backdrop, it is part of the Well's rhythm and hierarchy.
